# Council of the Guild · The Pivot Scouts

Three scouts were asked: is there a better product hiding in this engine? All three answered
**fold-in, not pivot** — and each brought back one organ worth harvesting. Part of the [v7 plan](../../PLAN.md).

## WAKE THE ARTIFACT — The Eleven Sigils. A secret progression layer over the existing orrery: every world hides one sigil earned by *noticing*, the Artifact is the lock, eleven sigils open a twelfth destination — Sector 00, the inside of the sphere.

*The Game Designer — MITHRIL's pivot scout. I don't pitch from vibes; I read /home/user/ddl-orrery/src/05-core.js and src/06-worlds.js first, and my job on this council is to say whether a playable frame beats the tour frame for the founder's actual goal (jaw-drop, show-not-tell), including saying "no pivot" if that's the truth. — recommendation: **hybrid-fold-in***

The Orrery already whispers a game it never lets you play: setScene() runs markSurveyed(), the #survey-log runs a completion rite with a gold seal, and touchArtifact() parts the plates every third touch to flash the biomech skin underneath for exactly 2600ms — a mystery it shows and then takes back. Wake the Artifact makes that flash the true ending. Each world's existing signature moment becomes a sigil earned by attention, not arrival: click the Dust Sea colossus mid-breach, hold perfectly still while the Abyssal leviathan passes ("the deep holds its breath" — stillness is the verb), tap the Grid's white trace where it's *going* to land, hand-light the seventh pyre the moment the sixth catches so the Beacons chain never breaks; the five worlds that already own a toy button (#run-query, #consult-archive, #call-drill, #brace-wall, #light-beacons) get their toys upgraded into tests with a win condition. Every sigil etches a glyph onto that world's Artifact skin, and with all eleven, one touch at the hub resolves the score's slightly-wrong answering tone (196 sagging to 185 in artifactAnswer()) into a true fifth, parts the plates permanently, and opens Sector 00 on the orbit ring — one designed payoff scene inside the machine, where the no-frameworks manifesto reads as diegetic engraving. No fail states, no score, no HUD gamification: miss a window and the world simply breathes again in thirty seconds. A website that was secretly a lock, and you were secretly the key.

**What carries over.** Effectively the entire engine and all assets. The shared Ticker, hash router, travel()/warp, and scene system in src/05-core.js untouched; all eleven WorldFX scenes and their state machines in src/06-worlds.js (each already tracks the event timers the sigil windows need — the breach, the leviathan pass, the trace, the beacon chain in bcnRun()); PlanetForge and SphereForge whole, including the skin crossfade machine (setSkin/reveal) which becomes the lock's visual language; the generative score in src/07-audio.js including artifactAnswer(), fanfare(), and the event-bus listeners; the Surveyor's Log as the game's existing tier-1 loop (visit = survey) with sigils as tier 2 (rings around the existing survey-dots); the Grand Tour stays a pure demo — sigils are gesture-gated, so autopilot can't earn them, which is correct; the no-JS brochure, reduced-motion end-states, and aria-hidden-canvas rules all stand.

**What must be built.** (1) New fragment src/05d-sigils.js (~250 lines): sigil registry keyed like WORLDS, localStorage persistence ('orrery-sigils' — must outlive the session, unlike the sessionStorage survey), listens on Orrery.events, dispatches 'sigil' and 'unlocked'. (2) Per-world window hooks in src/06-worlds.js: each FX state exposes an open-window flag + hit test (~20–40 lines per world; five worlds only need their existing button handler to check a condition instead of just firing). (3) The accessibility spine: a real DOM .w-sigil button in each world-card that arms when the window opens — canvas stays scenery, keyboard/AT players play the same game of attention; under reduced motion the windows arm on the world's slow clock instead of an animation frame (11 designed rules, real work, non-negotiable). (4) SphereForge: an etched-glyph overlay pass per skin and a permanent-reveal state (revealUntil = Infinity). (5) Sector 00: one new DOM section in src/04-body.html (deep-link gated — unearned it renders the sealed door, and in the no-JS brochure it reads as a locked epilogue, which is honest), one canvas scene reusing the biomech material, CSS in 03b-deco. (6) Score: a sigil chime per world voice and the resolved artifact answer. (7) One line of hub copy so the game is discoverable — the artifact-label swaps to 'Object 0 · it noticed you' after the first sigil.

**The honest tradeoff.** Honest ledger. AGAINST: the current orrery wins the first ten seconds, and the first ten seconds are what a skimming hiring manager or client sees — a progression layer pays off only for visitors who stay ten-plus minutes, and most never will, so if this costs anything from the skim (extra chrome, tutorial text, a score counter) it's a net loss for the stated goal. Discoverability is a knife edge: too subtle and the build is wasted on an audience of nobody; too loud and it reads as gamification chrome, killing the 'show, not tell' elegance. Scope is a full v-major (~1.5–2k lines plus a new scene plus 11 reduced-motion window designs), and the mood risk is real — Abyssal and Stormwall are dread pieces, and turning them into fidget targets would break exactly what makes them portfolio-grade (hence stillness-as-verb, one-shot interactions, no fail states). FOR: a secret game is the strongest possible show-not-tell — it demonstrates state machines, input design, audio-reactive systems, and restraint simultaneously, without one word of self-praise; it converts the piece's one genuine weakness (worlds are watched, not touched — only 5 of 11 have any verb at all) into its depth; and it gives the piece a second life in the exact channels that compound portfolio value (the person who finds Sector 00 posts about it). Versus a full pivot to 'a game': that would sacrifice the no-JS brochure, the LCP discipline, and the accessibility posture that make this credible as *web* craft — the orrery frame is the stronger chassis and should not be replaced, only armed.

## MUSICA UNIVERSALIS — "Conduct mode": the orrery becomes a playable celestial sequencer

*The Instrument Maker — MITHRIL's pivot scout. I read the engine looking for the product hiding inside the product, and I found one: the score registry and the orbit math are two halves of an instrument that have never been introduced to each other. — recommendation: **hybrid-fold-in***

The hub already has eleven voices (the ARR table in 07-audio.js gives every world a root, scale, 16-step motif, and perc pattern) and eleven moving bodies on parametric orbits — Conduct mode simply lets them play each other. A meridian line drops from the Artifact; every time a world crosses it, its motif strikes — so the orbit IS the loop, and because planetPos() already gives each world its own period ((95 + i*16)s today), the hub becomes a giant polyrhythmic step sequencer you can see. You ARRANGE by dragging planets between concentric orbit rings (closer ring = faster loop = higher octave — Kepler's third law as a music-theory joke), mute/solo by tapping the anchor labels, and the Artifact conducts: its sonar becomes the downbeat, touching it transposes the whole ensemble, and the every-third-touch reveal becomes the bridge section. Your arrangement serializes into the URL hash (eleven worlds × ring + mute fits in ~30 characters), so every visitor can compose a piece of the cosmos and send the link — a share loop the current orrery completely lacks. Time-of-day already tilts the sky (seasonTilt reads the local hour); we extend that so dawn visits play in major, midnight in minor, and optionally let keyless public feeds breathe through it (Open-Meteo rain drives Stormwall's taiko density, the USGS quake GeoJSON wakes the Dust Sea worm, wheretheiss.at draws one real satellite crossing the starfield that rings a bell when it passes) — all fetch-with-timeout, all silently falling back to today's procedural behavior offline. It's "music of the spheres" as a literal, playable trope: show-not-tell for WebAudio scheduling, pointer capture, canvas z-sorting, and URL-state design, without a single framework.

**What carries over.** Nearly everything, which is why this is buildable: the shared Ticker (self-removing tasks, idle-stop), the sky canvas + starfield + nebulae, PlanetForge sprites, SphereForge and all Artifact skins/behaviors (ping, ripple, reveal, setSkin), all eleven WorldFX scenes and travel/warp/router — click still warps, worlds stay destinations. The entire audio substrate carries over untouched: the 8-node persistent master chain, the self-cleaning note()/taiko()/shaker() transient voices, the ARR musical data, the gesture-gated ignition with the honest label, and the localStorage opt-out. Orrery.events is already the strike bus — the score answers 'preview' with two notes today; 'strike' is the same pattern with velocity. The Grand Tour's TourController is the exact structural precedent for the mode: a footer toggle, a Ticker task, cancel-on-input, aria-live plate. The survey log's completion rite (maybeCompletionRite in 05-core.js) is the perfect unlock hook: survey all eleven worlds, the log seals gold, and the baton appears — the instrument as the reward for the journey.

**What must be built.** Five real work items. (1) A Conductor module (new 07b fragment): a global tempo grid that quantizes each ARR's step to a shared subdivision — today arcadia steps at 170ms while uncharted steps at 900ms in a different key, and they have never had to coexist; the conductor must transpose all roots to one tonal center and snap steps to the grid, or the ensemble is mud. This is genuine music-theory work, not plumbing. (2) Score polyphony with a hard budget: buildRig costs up to 21 nodes, so eleven simultaneous rigs is phone death — cap armed drone/pad rigs at 3, let every other world contribute only self-cleaning strike notes (note() already disconnects onended, so strikes scale fine). (3) Orbit model: replace the single ART.rx/ry ellipse with 3-4 concentric rings; period derived from ring; pointer-capture drag on .planet-anchor (which already tracks --pax/--pay/--psize) plus arrow-keys-on-focused-anchor for keyboard parity; drag must not fight tap-to-travel on coarse pointers (the finePointer guard exists — press-and-hold to lift a planet on touch). (4) Hub UI: a Conduct toggle beside #tour-toggle, mute/solo chips on pa-labels, hash serialization #/score/… beside the existing #/world/ routes. (5) The data whisperers: three fetch wrappers with AbortController timeouts that feed existing parameters (worm cadence, taiko density, one new sky streak) and vanish silently offline. Reduced motion keeps its law: orbits hold their seeded pose, and strikes fire on the conductor's clock instead of crossings — the instrument degrades to a step grid, a designed end-state, never a stripped one. No-JS is untouched; Conduct mode is JS-only like the Tour.

**The honest tradeoff.** Honestly weighed against the current orrery for jaw-drop-and-show-not-tell: FOR — the orrery is watched, an instrument is played, and participation is the strongest jaw-drop there is; the shareable-composition hash gives it a viral loop the current piece lacks; polyrhythmic WebAudio lookahead scheduling synced to canvas orbits demonstrates far rarer engineering than an ambient score; and it deepens the two best systems already in the repo instead of bolting on a twelfth world. AGAINST — three sharp risks. First, audio is gesture-gated and opt-out-remembered by design (07-audio's LAW), so a meaningful slice of visitors will never hear the instrument's whole point; the strike visuals (ring flash, planet ripple, --acc retint) must be composed to carry the piece silently, which is extra design work the current orrery doesn't need. Second, a bad generative mix is worse than silence: eleven motifs in five different scales at six different tempos will sound like a mall food court unless the conductor's key/tempo unification is genuinely good — this is the highest-skill, highest-risk task and it cannot be faked. Third, the hub's current power is restraint: a vast dark, a slow sonar, an artifact that answers. Bolting a mixer onto it risks trading dread for clutter — which is exactly why Conduct mode must be a discovered second act (unlocked by the survey seal), not the front door. A full pivot would also orphan the proven showpiece — the warp, the eleven world scenes, the tour — betting everything on a channel some users have muted.

## SIGNAL ZERO — a diegetic mystery layered over the existing orrery: the Artifact is broadcasting, and each of the 11 worlds holds one fragment of the answer.

*The Showman — MITHRIL's pivot scout. My job this council is to find the alternative product hiding inside the engine, pitch it at full strength, and then be honest about whether it should replace the orrery or be organ-harvested by it. — recommendation: **hybrid-fold-in***

The Artifact has been sounding its sonar this whole time — SIGNAL ZERO makes that a question the visitor can actually answer. Each world hides one fragment of the signal, and you earn it by doing exactly what the world already invites: send the pulse in Aurora, be watching when the worm breaches the Dust Sea, light the beacon chain, consult the Archive at the moment the futures collapse. The surveyor's log stops being a checklist and becomes a cipher strip filling in glyph by glyph, and the hub subtly registers your progress — the sonar cadence tightens, the reveal lingers a beat longer. Decode all eleven and the finale fires: the plates part and stay parted, the score resolves the 'slightly-wrong answering tone' into consonance, and the colossus — the creature the konami code teased — arrives in the hub sky, answering the call. Nothing is ever explained in copy; the page never says 'quest' or 'achievement.' It is the purest possible show-not-tell: the site itself is the puzzle box, and the reward is a set-piece the engine already knows how to draw.

**What carries over.** Essentially everything — that is the point of the pitch. The shared Ticker and idle-draining rAF discipline, drawSky's z-sorted orbit pass with SphereForge shadow/sonar interleave (05-core.js:258-298), PlanetForge and all 11 WorldFX modules untouched as renderers, the warp/travel/hash-router flow, SphereForge's full API (setSkin per-world faces, ripple, ping, and especially reveal — which becomes the finale's held-open state instead of a 2.6 s tease), the generative score in 07-audio.js including its deliberately wrong artifact tone (now a promise the finale keeps), the surveyor's log plumbing (markSurveyed, the 'scene' event, the dots-and-count plate at 05-core.js:475-536) as the direct substrate for the cipher strip, the colossus flyby geometry and flyTask from 08-konami.js as the finale set-piece, the Grand Tour, the approach cinematic, and every house rule: the quest is JS-only progressive enhancement exactly like the survey log already is, so no-JS remains the complete stacked brochure and reduced-motion earns fragments on designed dwell-timers instead of motion events.

**What must be built.** Five concrete work items. (1) A new fragment 05d-signal.js: a state module owning the fragment set (sessionStorage via the existing store wrapper), listening for a new CustomEvent('fragment', {slug}) on Orrery.events, and running the finale rite when the set hits 11. (2) One dispatch line at each world's existing climax in 06-worlds.js — the toys (#run-query, #consult-archive, #call-drill, #brace-wall, #light-beacons) and the timed set-pieces (worm breach in fx.dune, leviathan pass, the Grid trace arriving, the Velocity riders lapping, the Uncharted draft reaching a threshold) already have the exact code points; each needs an rm/dwell fallback so reduced-motion players are never locked out. (3) Extract the colossus flyby from the 08-konami.js IIFE into a shared callable (or fire it via an Orrery event) so both the konami rite and the finale can summon it; the finale variant flies toward the Artifact rather than across. (4) Upgrade the survey-log plate in 03b-deco.css: .survey-dot becomes an 11-slot glyph strip with a distinct 'decoded' state layered on 'surveyed', plus finale-state CSS keyed off an html-level class. (5) One diegetic hint line per world card in the existing .w-note--live microlabel voice ('Watch for the white trace' is already the correct register), and a per-fragment motif plus final consonance resolution in 07-audio.js. Deliberate scope cut: the Grand Tour surveys but does not decode — autopilot must not spoil the hands-on game, and that distinction gives the tour a clean identity as the demo mode.

**The honest tradeoff.** Full pivot vs current orrery, honestly scored for portfolio jaw-drop: the pivot loses. The orrery's opening shot — eleven procedural worlds z-sorted around a reactive gold sphere, occlusion-dimmed labels, the sonar ring — lands in the first five seconds with zero effort from the visitor; a puzzle-gated frame hides that behind engagement the median recruiter or client will never give (visitors budget ~90 seconds), and it adds failure states the current piece doesn't have: 'am I stuck?', 'is it broken?', 'how much is left?'. A true ARG also wants a community and server state, and the hard constraints (static Pages, no backend) cap it at a single-visitor toy, which is the weakest kind of ARG. The hybrid costs roughly 15-20% new scope and two real risks: over-cluing (the microlabels start reading as tutorial copy and the show-not-tell voice breaks) and turning delight into homework (fragments must be earned by doing what each world already invites, never by pixel-hunting). What the hybrid buys: the current piece's one genuine weakness is that it is a gorgeous menu — nothing pulls a visitor past world three, and the mystery props (reveal, sonar, colossus, the wrong tone) are dead ends that read as unfinished to a careful reviewer. The signal spine converts the collection into a story with an earned finale, and — for the portfolio audience specifically — quietly demonstrates the hardest skills to show off: cross-module event architecture, long-lived state machines over a shared clock, and musical resolution, all without a single line of copy calling them out.
