## Sector 01 · Dust Sea — *Desert giant*

**Critique.** The bones are disciplined: three precomputed Float32Array ridges with sinusoidal drift (drawRidge, src/06-worlds.js:155-162), 78 pooled sand streaks stroked in a single pass with a ~22s gust sine (clock*0.00028, line 143), and correct occlusion — the worm is drawn between ridges[1] and ridges[2] so the front dune hides its lower body (line 131). But the worm itself, our entire reason to exist, is 9–13 plain filled circles (g.arc, fill rgba(11,6,19,0.96), lines 113-121) arching 122–168px regardless of viewport — the card promises "Something vast turns beneath the dunes" (src/04-body.html:135) and delivers a chain of dark beads with no maw, no plates, no rim light, no camera reaction, and no sound: unlike aurora's 'query' event (06-worlds.js:794), fx.dune dispatches nothing, so the Score's otherwise-perfect Phrygian-dominant taiko arrangement (07-audio.js:36-42) plays straight through the breach as if nothing happened. Cadence is also stingy: first breach at 9–17s (line 88), then 26–44s gaps (line 111), so the median visitor sees exactly one small silent event and leaves. Dust Sea is one of the only worlds with no player verb — no w-toy button (aurora, archive, drillyard, stormwall, and beacons all have one) and no pointer handling (uncharted tracks pointermove at 06-worlds.js:808) — which is absurd for the one trope built on thumpers and walking without rhythm. The two SVG suns (04-body.html:121-122) never move, so there is no second act for anyone who dwells. And the reduced-motion/no-JS end-state (.ds-static, 03-worlds.css:194-198) is two bare dune paths: the world's signature creature simply does not exist for those visitors, which is against the house's own "designed end-states, never stripped scenes" law. Interest half-life today is roughly 40 seconds; the scene is a competent screensaver wearing a Dune costume.

### The signature moment

You read the caption — "the dunes are not as empty as they look" — and press PLANT A THUMPER. A stake drives into the front dune and starts pounding: with every piston strike a dust ring blooms and a fat 49Hz thump lands in the score, dead-synced, lump-lump, lump-lump. Four beats in, the music thins — the drone ducks, a sub-bass rumble creeps up, and the sand grains around the thumper start to jump on each pulse. Then you see it: a moving mound swelling the front ridge at the screen's edge, racing toward your thumper like a bow wave. The rumble peaks, the taiko double-hits, the camera judders — and a rim-lit leviathan erupts through the dune at half the screen's height, three mandibles hinging open against the twin-sun sky, plated body cascading sand as it arcs over the exact spot you chose and takes the thumper down with it. The pounding stops mid-beat. Silence, settling dust — and along the scar of its dive, a field of spice glitters cyan and amber in the last light. You planted a lure, the desert answered, and it answered YOU. That's the story a visitor tells: "I called the worm."

### The upgrades

- **The Thumper — plant a lure, call the worm** — *🌟 jaw-drop · effort L*
  A w-toy button (and direct tap on the dunes) plants a pounding thumper stake. Each piston strike emits a dust ring and a synced sub-bass thump in the score. The next breach is no longer random: worm-sign races toward YOUR thumper and the worm devours it at the exact spot you chose. The one Dune verb everyone knows, made playable.
  *How:* Add <button class="w-toy" id="plant-thumper"> to the dust-sea card mirroring #call-drill (04-body.html:414), plus pointerdown on the section's .world-fx for direct placement (x from clientX vs canvas rect; y snapped to ridges[2] surface using the same idx math as the worm spawner at 06-worlds.js:97). In fx.dune.init add st.thumper={on,x,y,beat}; draw a stake + bobbing piston head; every 900ms of clock spawn an expanding ring stroke + 2 spawnPuff() calls and dispatch Orrery.events CustomEvent('thump'). In 07-audio.js, next to the 'sonar' listener (line 506), add a 'thump' listener: sine 49Hz with exponential drop to 38Hz over 0.18s plus a noiseSrc() tick through a 300Hz lowpass, sent wet — gated on ready(), self-stopping like every other note. Steering: when thumper.on, replace bx = W*(0.28+rnd*0.44) (line 96) with bx = thumper.x and pull st.next to clock+3500; when the breach head passes within 30px of the stake, kill it with a 14-puff burst. Cleanup via the existing activeState.cleanup hook (06-worlds.js:44).

- **Walk Without Rhythm — the worm hunts your footsteps** — *🌟 jaw-drop · effort L*
  Your pointer leaves footprints in the sand below the horizon. Move with a steady, regular cadence and the desert notices: a rumble builds, grains jump around your trail, and the worm breaches at your last footprint and swallows the whole track. Move arrhythmically — deliberately jerky, stop-start — and its attention decays and it never comes. The caption flips to 'walk without rhythm.' Visitors will spend minutes deliberately failing.
  *How:* pointermove on the section (registered/removed exactly like fx.draft's at 06-worlds.js:806-809). Sample position every ~100ms into a 16-slot ring buffer; compute per-sample speeds; regularity = 1 - normalized variance; while speed > threshold, attention += regularity*dt, else decay. Footprints: pool of 24 dimple pairs (two small dark ellipses offset perpendicular to travel direction) stamped every 34px of travel when pointer y > H*0.5, fading over 8s. attention >= 1 triggers the existing wm breach aimed at the last footprint (set wm.xA/dir from it) plus the 'wormsign' event from the rumble upgrade. Swap the w-note textContent between the rhythm hint and 'you have its attention' — the card is real DOM so it stays honest copy. Coarse-pointer fallback: the thumper toy covers phones.

- **The Breach, made vast — from bead-chain to leviathan** — *🌟 jaw-drop · effort L*
  Replace the 13 dark circles with an actual worm: one continuous ribbed body with a tapering radius, crescent armor plates, a sun-side rim light, and a three-lobed maw that hinges open on the rise — scaled to half the viewport with a decaying camera judder and sand cascading off its back. The current render is the single biggest gap between promise ('something vast') and delivery.
  *How:* Keep the existing segment-center math (06-worlds.js:114-121) but build one closed Path2D: compute per-segment normals, radius profile 34px at head tapering to 12 at tail (scaled by H/640), trace the top edge out and bottom edge back, fill near-black. Overlay per-segment crescent arcs: dark stroke rgba(0,0,0,0.5) plus a rim stroke rgba(255,178,94,0.35) offset toward sun A's position (the SVG sun sits at 452,452 — 04-body.html:121). Maw: while p<0.5 draw three wedge mandibles rotated open by sin(p*PI), interior gradient to #1c1030. archH = min(H*0.5, 320). Shake: st.shake=1 at eruption, ctx.translate((rnd-0.5)*6*shake) each frame with shake *= exp(-dt/300). Bow wave: pass an optional bulge {x, amp} into drawRidge and add amp*exp(-((xs[i]-x)/60)^2) to each ys — the ridge arrays are already per-init Float32Arrays, untouched. Raise the puff pool 30 -> 60; still one fill style, one path, well within the mid-phone budget.

- **Worm-sign rumble — the score feels it coming (A/V sync)** — *◆ strong · effort M*
  Eight seconds before every breach, a mound swells and races along the front ridge toward the eruption point while the music ducks under a rising sub-bass rumble; sand grains hop on each rumble pulse; at eruption the taiko double-hits with a wet noise burst as the camera judders. Respects the no-pre-gesture law via the existing ready() gate.

- **Two suns actually set — a second act for dwellers** — *◆ strong · effort M*

- **Spice in the wake — harvest the bloom** — *· nice · effort M*

- **Sandwalk parallax — the dunes answer your gaze** — *· nice · effort S*

## Sector 02 · Velocity — *Neon metropolis*

**Critique.** Velocity is the prettiest static postcard in the orrery and the least alive world in it. The card copy in 04-body.html promises "the skyline re-renders on the orrery clock," but fx.velocity.frame just writes st.bars[i].style.setProperty('--h', 0.2 + Math.random()*0.72) every 2600ms — it is noise, not a chart, and the manifesto's line that "the skyline IS a live bar chart" charts nothing. The two riders from vMakeRider are 3.2px dots crawling constant-speed (0.34 vs 0.31) along fixed mirrored vBuildPath loops that never intersect, so the advertised "race" has no lead changes, no proximity, no story; the only canvas event is the orange window pulse every 7–12s, which reads as a glitch. Velocity is also the only heavily-themed world with zero player verb — aurora has #run-query, drillyard has #call-drill, storm has #brace-wall, uncharted tracks pointermove, while velocity's section has no .w-toy and no listener anywhere in 06-worlds.js. Trope-wise the biggest gaps are unforgivable for outrun: no rain, no wet-street reflections, no chase camera, and a .mesa-sun that never moves. The bones are genuinely good — compositor-only scaleY bars, the grid-run keyframe on .mesa-grid::before, pooled V_CAP trails, and ARR.velocity's driving 220ms ostinato with spark:true is the best backing track in the score — but the scene exhausts its interest in about fifteen seconds. Finally, .velocity-static (two polylines and two dots) is the weakest reduced-motion end-state in the file next to .aby-static's full leviathan tableau, which undercuts the house law that motion-off means a designed pose, not a stripped one.

### The signature moment

You've been watching the postcard for ten seconds — two dots dragging neon ribbons under a banded chrome sun — when you press and hold. The whole scene folds: the side-view flattens, the horizon rushes up, and suddenly you are ON the grid, first-person, cyan trail pouring out behind you, the skyline towers sliding past as equalizer columns literally pumping with the bassline. The magenta rival is right there, weaving two lanes ahead. You drift your pointer to slot into its slipstream, the lowpass on the score opens up like a throttle, and as you draw level the world drops into bullet-time — a quarter-speed near-miss, one white gap-frame flash as your trails cross, a pitch-dive stinger — then time snaps back, you release, and the camera pulls up and away to reveal the crossing you just survived written across the city in light. Reduced-motion visitors get that exact frozen instant as the scene's poster. That is the thing you text a friend about: "hold your finger on the neon city one. Trust me."

### The upgrades

- **Hold to Ride — the chase-camera drop** — *🌟 jaw-drop · effort XL*
  Press and hold anywhere in the scene (or hit a 'Ride shotgun' toy button) and the camera dives behind the cyan light-cycle: full first-person outrun road, towers rushing past, the magenta rival weaving ahead, steering with pointer-x or arrow keys. Release and it tweens back to the orbit view, your run's trail still glowing on the map.
  *How:* Add a mode scalar (0..1, tweened over ~700ms) to fx.velocity state; pointerdown>=350ms on .world-fx sets target 1, pointerup sets 0; a .w-toy button (same pattern as #call-drill) toggles it for keyboard/touch. At mode 1, frame() renders classic pseudo-3D road math: ~48 z-rows, screenY = horizon + k/z, roadHalfWidth = w0/z, curvature accumulated per row — ~120 lines, canvas only. Rival is a glowing quad using the same sine jink as vBuildPath. DOM layers ride along transform-only: an is-riding class scales .mesa-skyline 1.6x and fades .mesa-grid. Steering = pointer-x mapped to lane offset; off-road shakes via ctx.translate jitter. Dispatch CustomEvent('ride') on Orrery.events; in 07-audio.js the listener ramps the rig's drone lowpass with setTargetAtTime and doubles shaker density while held (engine-opens-up). state.cleanup removes listeners and forces mode 0; Orrery.reduced() disables the verb entirely and the card note points at the poster instead.

- **The crossover duel — bullet-time near-miss** — *🌟 jaw-drop · effort L*
  The riders' paths finally intersect. When both close on a shared crossing within a beat of each other, the world drops to quarter speed, the canvas punches in 8%, their trails cross in a single white gap-frame flash, and a pitch-dive stinger fires. First one is scripted to happen within 20 seconds of arrival, so every visitor sees it.
  *How:* Rebuild vBuildPath so the mirrored paths share two crossing columns (flip the jink sign on one rider at i===3 and i===7 so the yj excursions overlap instead of mirroring apart). In frame(), track each rider's arc-distance to the shared crossing point (seg[] prefix sums already exist); when both are <60px and closing, set state.timeScale=0.25 for 900ms and multiply dt locally — the shared Ticker is untouched, per house law. Zoom via ctx.translate/scale centered on the crossing; at minimum separation draw one frame of both riders as white silhouettes. Dispatch CustomEvent('nearmiss'); Score answers with a dry square note ramping root*4 down to root*2 over 300ms. Deterministically phase-offset the riders at init so the first crossover lands inside 20s. rm(): nothing to do — see the poster upgrade, which freezes exactly this instant.

- **The skyline becomes an honest chart — a 26-band equalizer of the score** — *◆ strong · effort M*
  The manifesto says the skyline IS a live bar chart; make it true. With sound on, the 26 towers are a real-time spectrum analyzer of the generative score. With sound off, they dance to the silent motif on the same 220ms grid — so enabling audio is a reveal: the city was already playing the music you couldn't hear.
  *How:* Expose Score.analyser(): a lazy getter in 07-audio.js that creates one AnalyserNode (fftSize 128, smoothing 0.75) tapped off comp, returns null pre-gesture — no change to the 8-node persistent graph budget. In fx.velocity.frame, every 2nd frame call getByteFrequencyData into one reusable Uint8Array, map 26 log-spaced bins to --h via setProperty — still transform-only scaleY, but add an is-eq class dropping the bar transition from 1.4s to ~90ms. Fallback when !Score.on: derive stepIdx from ticker clock (clock/220|0) and drive bars from ARR.velocity's motif/perc tables deterministically. Delete the Math.random() re-render block (st.nextRender) and rewrite the card's .w-note--live so the copy stops overselling. rm() keeps its existing deterministic pose.

- **Back a rider — laps, tap-to-slipstream, photo-finish** — *◆ strong · effort L*
  Two team chips in the world card: BACK CYN / BACK MAG. The race gains laps, a checkered gate, and drama: your rider surges when you tap the scene, rubber-banding keeps it close, and a sub-30px finish triggers a photo-finish white flash and a running tally ('LAP 4 · CYN 3–1 MAG') that persists between visits.
  *How:* Two .w-toy buttons (house convention from #call-drill / #brace-wall). In frame(): per-lap pace variance of ±8% on r.speed, rubber-band up to +6% for the trailing rider, and a 400ms +25% surge for the backed rider on scene pointerdown (throttled 1/s). Finish gate is a canvas checkered strip at x=w*0.5; on leader crossing with gap<30px, draw one 120ms full-canvas white fillRect fade, hold both riders 300ms, update the .w-note--live text node and a localStorage tally (same persistence pattern as the surveyor's log in 05-core.js). Dispatch CustomEvent('photofinish'); Score plays the two-note preview figure at ARR.velocity's root. html:not(.js) hides the chips like every other toy; reduced motion keeps chips but resolves races instantly with the text tally only.

- **Rain-slick streets — the reflection layer** — *◆ strong · effort M*
  The missing half of the trope: sparse two-tone neon rain, and a wet street that mirrors the skyline and both rider trails in shimmering, jittered reflections. The scene instantly doubles in depth and reads unmistakably as Blade Runner asphalt.
  *How:* Define a street band (bottom ~22% of canvas). After drawing trails/riders, replay them mirrored: g.translate(0, 2*streetY); g.scale(1,-1); globalAlpha 0.28; reuse vDrawTrail with a precomputed 2px vertical offset table for wet shimmer (no per-point sin). DOM skyline reflection: wrap bars so --h is set on a shared parent, add #mesa-skyline-r styled scaleY(-1), blur(2px), opacity .25, mask-image fade — both layers read the same custom properties, zero extra JS writes. Rain: 60 pooled streaks (house pooling style, caps documented in the header comment) falling at 78 degrees, cyan/magenta alternating; on hitting streetY spawn a 3-frame ripple ellipse from a 12-slot pool. Reduced motion: no rain, but reflections are baked into the upgraded static poster so the end-state keeps the depth.

- **Beat-locked city — every event lands on the 220ms grid** — *◆ strong · effort M*
  Nothing in the scene fires at random anymore: the orange window pulse detonates on the taiko K, the grid horizontals pop on the downbeat, bars dip-and-recover on kicks, riders spark on shakers. The whole city becomes visibly quantized to the score — and stays quantized, silently, when audio is off.
  *How:* In armSched's while loop in 07-audio.js, after scheduling each step dispatch CustomEvent('step', {detail:{at: performance.now() + (nextNote - ctx.currentTime)*1000, idx: stepIdx, perc: pc}}) — converting audio clock to perf clock so the rAF world can align. fx.velocity keeps a small ring buffer of pending steps; frame() consumes those whose at <= clock: 'K' replaces the random st.pulseNext timer for the window pulse and writes a one-frame --gridpop brightness property on .mesa-grid; 's' emits a 1-frame headlight spark on the leader. Fallback: when !Score.on, synthesize the identical 16-step/220ms grid from ticker clock so structure exists before sound is granted. Event rate is ~4.5Hz — negligible. Reduced motion: no change needed; the FX never runs under html.rm.

- **The frozen frame is a poster, not a leftover** — *· nice · effort M*
  Reduced-motion and no-JS visitors currently get two bent lines and two dots. Replace .velocity-static with a full synthwave poster: skyline silhouette with lit windows, banded sun, both trails frozen mid-crossover at the near-miss instant, wet-street reflections, checkered gate. The end-state becomes the scene's definitive image, not its consolation prize.
  *How:* Pure SVG in 04-body.html plus a few rules in 03-worlds.css — no JS, matching how .aby-static carries the abyssal tableau. Skyline as a run of <rect>s with 2-3px window dots, sun reusing the existing scanline mask trick, trails as two <path>s with <linearGradient> stroke fades crossing at center (the exact composition of the crossover duel), a scaleY(-1) low-opacity <use> of the trail group below a street line for reflections. Extend the existing display toggles (html.rm / html:not(.js) rules already in 03-worlds.css lines ~210-214) unchanged. Also keep rm()'s deterministic bar pose and pose the new reflection layer with it. This is the item that pays the 'designed end-states, never stripped scenes' law in full.

- **Dusk engine — the chrome sun actually sets** — *· nice · effort S*
  Over about two minutes of dwell, the banded sun sinks through its own scanlines, the sky shifts magenta to deep violet, tower windows brighten as daylight dies, and the riders' trails become the brightest thing in the world. Long-dwell visitors get a second act instead of a loop.
  *How:* One scene-local cycle scalar written at 2Hz from the world's frame(): html-level custom property --dusk (0..1, 120s period, eased). No-JS: the CSS default is the same frozen pose.

## Sector 03 · The Grid — *Cyberspace*

**Critique.** The renderer is the most technically elegant thing about the scene and the least visible: fx.grid in src/06-worlds.js never clears the canvas, using a persistent G_OVER = rgba(2,18,10,0.12) fade fill as the trail engine, holding to ~40 fillText calls per frame across a 64-column cap — genuinely smart, and it reads as the single most default Matrix rain on the internet. The card copy promises "Watch for the white trace. It knows where it is going," but the trace is C[(Math.random()*n)|0] painted white once for 520ms every 9-15 seconds; it knows nothing, goes nowhere, and most visitors will blink through it. The Grid is the only trope-world among its peers with zero player verb — aurora has #run-query firing a CustomEvent('query') the score answers, uncharted drafts under pointermove, archive/drillyard/stormwall/beacons all ship w-toy buttons — while the one trope whose entire mythology is about choice (red pill, blue pill) offers the visitor nothing to do. It also has no set-piece rhythm: dust-sea gets THE WORM on a 26-44s cadence, abyssal gets a 14-second leviathan that dims the light shafts, and the Grid's "event" is a half-second recolor. The score (E-minor add9 plucks, triangle lead, delay 0.55) is lovely and completely blind to the visuals — no event coupling exists in either direction. Finally, .grid-static in src/03-worlds.css:237 is the weakest reduced-motion end-state in the file — bare repeating-gradient stripes — where abyssal ships a full designed SVG tableau, and fx.grid is one of the few effects with no rm() hook at all. Interest half-life today: about fifteen seconds.

### The signature moment

You press and hold anywhere on the rain — and time obeys. Within a fingertip's radius the glyphs decelerate, then hang mid-air, a frozen sphere of code bending around your touch like light around a lens, while the score's delay feedback swells and the plucks smear down a detuned half-step, the whole room holding its breath. Then you see it: the white trace, the one the card told you about, stops falling, turns, and starts threading sideways across the frozen columns — column to column, deliberate, unmistakably coming for YOU. It arrives at your fingertip and every suspended glyph inside the sphere flips white at once, ringing a circle of readable characters around your finger: THERE IS NO SPOON — mirrored the same instant in a real-DOM caption below the card. You let go. The sphere snaps shut with a whip-crack ripple through all 64 columns and one bright octave spark in the score, and the rain falls on as if it never happened. Nine seconds later, it happens again. Exactly the same. Déjà vu.

### The upgrades

- **There Is No Spoon — hold to bend time** — *🌟 jaw-drop · effort M*
  Press-and-hold (mouse or touch) anywhere in the rain: glyphs inside a growing radius slow, then freeze mid-fall, columns bow outward around the pointer like a lens; the music smears — delay feedback swells, pad detunes a half-step down. Release and the sphere snaps shut with a ripple that whips through every column and a single octave spark in the score. Bullet time as a verb.
  *How:* In fx.grid.init, add pointerdown/up/move listeners on s.c.parentElement.parentElement (the .world-fx wrapper, same pattern as fx.draft's pointermove with st.cleanup teardown). Per frame, for each of the <=64 columns compute d = dist(c.x, c.y, px, py); inside radius R (eased up over ~600ms of hold), scale c.acc accumulation by t = smoothstep(d/R) so step timers dilate to zero at the center, and draw heads at x + dx where dx is a radial displacement (max ~14px). Frozen glyphs must survive the G_OVER fade: repaint any column with t<0.3 each frame in G_TRAIL after the fade fill (bounded extra fillText, still under budget). On release, run a 400ms spring on dx and a one-shot expanding ring stroke. Audio: dispatch CustomEvent('bend', {detail:{held:true|false}}) on window.Orrery.events; in 07-audio.js add a listener beside the existing 'query' hook that ramps the shared delay feedback gain node up to ~0.7 and applies detune -80 cents to the live pad oscillators, reversing on release plus one bright pluck. ## Sector 11 · The Beacons — *Watch-fire range*

**Critique.** The craft floor is genuinely high: `fx.beacons` (src/06-worlds.js:1621) builds five ridged-noise silhouettes with per-ridge parallax drift, seven pyres with a three-layer flame in `bcnPyre`, a prebaked 64px glow sprite composited 'lighter', pooled embers (96) and smoke (26), wind gusts, and stars that sharpen between signals via `st.calm` — and the rm/no-JS story (`.beacon-static` SVG frozen mid-burn, `bcn-flare-rm` at src/03-worlds.css:429) is house-rule exemplary. But the scene is a screensaver wearing a costume: the chain auto-runs every ~22s (`BCN_GAP` at line 1571, fired unconditionally at line 1682), so the one player verb — the `#light-beacons` button — merely skips a wait; there is no ownership, no cost, no way to fail. Worse, the toy's `CustomEvent('beacon')` (line 1776) is a dead wire: 07-audio.js listens for preview/warp/scene/query/konami/sonar/artifact and never 'beacon', so the seven ignitions — timed to the millisecond by `st.sig.t0 + i*BCN_STAGGER` — are completely silent while the generic pentatonic motif noodles on, uncorrelated. The pyres are anonymous (the trope's whole power is that Amon Dîn and Eilenach are *named places* answering each other), the camera never moves off the single wide shot so all drama happens at 6px flame scale, and nothing varies between runs. Interest half-life is one auto-run plus one click: about thirty seconds. Everything needed to fix it is already lying on the workbench — a knowable ignition schedule, an event bus, a `note()` synth helper, and a pointer-event precedent at line 808.

### The signature moment

You press and HOLD "Light the beacons." Sparks skitter off the flint — tick, tick — the kindling glows, almost gutters, then catches with a whump and a low horn. And then the camera leaves the ground: the view lifts off the first hillside and flies down the range, ridge silhouettes sliding past at different speeds, as fire answers fire — each ignition a shockwave of light rim-lighting its valley, each horn call a step higher and a room farther away, each peak's name kindling in the roll-call: Dunharrow's answer, then the next, then the next. At the seventh pyre the horns and the score cut to silence for one full breath — embers drifting, stars sharp — and then, impossibly far beyond the last ridge, an eighth point of light you never noticed flickers on, and a single distant horn answers from somewhere off the map. The readout prints: "…and the answer comes." Your friend asks you to do it again, and this time a rain squall is crossing peak four, and you have to cup your hand over the flame to keep the signal alive.
